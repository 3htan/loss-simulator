    How it works

    The aggregate loss model behind the numbers, as it appears on actuarial exams.

    Aggregate loss is a random sum

    A year's total loss adds up a random number of random claims. The count N and each claim size Xi have their own distributions, and the claim sizes are independent of each other and of N.

    S = X₁ + X₂ + … + XN, S = 0 if N = 0

    When N is Poisson, S has a compound Poisson distribution. It has a point mass at zero, P(S = 0) = e−λ, which is the spike in the first bar of the chart.

    Moments come from N and X

    Conditioning on N gives the general results. For Poisson, Var(N) = E[N] = λ, so they simplify.

    E[S] = E[N]·E[X] = λ·E[X]
    Var(S) = E[N]·Var(X) + Var(N)·E[X]² = λ·E[X²]

    Percentiles of S have no simple formula. That is why simulation (or Panjer recursion, or a normal approximation) is used to find VaR.

    Deductible and limit act on each claim

    With an ordinary deductible d and a policy limit u on the payment, the insurer pays per claim:

    Y = min( max(X − d, 0), u )

    The loss elimination ratio measures how much of the expected loss the deductible removes:

    LER = E[X ∧ d] / E[X]

    A claim hits the limit when X ≥ d + u. The insurer's annual payment is the sum of the Y's.

    What one simulated year does

    1. Draw N from Poisson(λ).
    2. Draw N claim sizes from the severity distribution.
    3. Add them for the ground-up S; apply d and u to each and add for the insurer's total.
    Repeat for every year, sort the totals, and read off the mean, standard deviation and percentiles.

    VaRp is the p-th percentile of S: the loss exceeded in a share 1 − p of years. VaR95% is the "1-in-20-year" loss.

    Exam tie-in: frequency/severity models, coverage modifications, LER and aggregate loss models are core material on SOA Exam FAM and ASTAM, and on CAS MAS-I. Try setting the deductible to 0 and the limit off: the insurer and ground-up columns should match exactly.
